Create custom resin soap dishes and bathroom accessories with this step-by-step guide covering mold making, pouring, coloring, and finishing for durable bathroom pieces.

Resin Soap Dishes And Bathroom Set: DIY Guide

Resin soap dishes and bathroom accessories combine functional utility with artistic expression. This guide walks you through the complete process from material selection to final finishing, with practical details you can verify at home.

Materials and Preparation

Before starting, gather these materials:

  • Epoxy resin system: 1:1 or 2:1 mixing ratio by weight (hardener + resin), depending on product. Use a casting resin rated for thin pours (1/4″ or less) as specified by the manufacturer. Pro tip: System Three SeaCast, Alumite Amazing Clear Cast, and ArtResin are popular choices for decorative pours frequently referenced in resin art communities.
  • Silicone mold putty or liquid silicone: Flexible silicone for easy demolding. Pour-around molds or bead molds work well for flat soap dish forms.
  • Pigment powders or liquid resin dyes: For color. Mica powders in metallic or opaque finishes show wear well in bathroom environments.
  • Glitter or fine decorative flakes: Optional, for visual effect. Add at tacky stage.
  • Mold release spray: For easy demolding.
  • Protective equipment: Nitrile gloves, respirator (VOCs from resin), and safety glasses.
  • Heat gun or torch: For bubble removal.
  • Sandpaper: 400-grit, 600-grit, and 800-grit wet/dry sandpaper.
  • Automotive polishing compound: For final polish.
  • Clear acrylic sealer or UV-resistant clear coat: For pieces facing windows or outdoor elements.
  • Plastic plant pot or template: As an inner form to hold soap and water.

Creating the Mold

  1. Prepare your design: Decide on the soap dish shape and size. Standard soap dishes range 4-6″ wide and 3-4″ deep, but customize to your sink layout. Sketch the shape on paper at actual size, including drainage channels.

  2. Mix silicone mold putty: Follow the manufacturer’s ratio (typically 1:1 by weight or volume). Mix thoroughly until uniform color with no streaks. For liquid silicone, weigh base and catalyst separately.

  3. Create the mold impression: Press your design into the putty, or pour putty around a plastic template. Ensure at least 1/4″ thickness around the entire design for durability. If using a two-part pour, align halves carefully before the putty sets.

  4. Embed decorative elements: Before the putty fully cures, press in decorative embedments like colored glass chips, shells, or polished stones at the surface. Hold in place until set—typically 5-10 minutes depending on product.

  5. Let cure fully: Follow the putty manufacturer’s cure time, typically 30 minutes to 2 hours before demolding, 24 hours for full strength.

  6. Demold carefully: Flex the silicone to release the resin blank. If the planter sticks, place the mold in the freezer for 10-15 minutes—cold contracts the silicone and releases the piece. Do not pry with hard objects.

Mixing and Pouring Resin

  1. Weigh resin and hardener: Digital scale accurate to 0.1g. Exactly the manufacturer-specified ratio by weight. For a typical 6″ soap dish, 100-200g total resin is sufficient depending on wall thickness.

  2. Stir slowly: Stir for 3 minutes, scraping sides and bottom of the cup. Fast stirring introduces bubbles that are hard to remove in thin pours.

  3. Add color: Mix pigment powder at 2-5% by weight of total resin, or add liquid dye drop by drop. Mix thoroughly. For bathroom pieces, opaque or metallic colors often show water spots less than translucent.

  4. Optional: Add glitter: If using, add a pinch of fine glitter while resin is still mixed—glitter sinks quickly, so mix quickly after adding.

  5. Pour into mold: Pour slowly into the deepest point of the mold to avoid air entrapment. Fill to the desired wall thickness, but don’t overfill.

  6. Bubble removal: Immediately after pouring, use a heat gun or torch on low setting, moving constantly, to pop surface bubbles. Keep the heat moving to avoid local overheating, especially in thin pours.

  7. Let reach tacky stage: Depending on resin system and ambient temperature (75-85°F ideal), resin will reach a tacky stage in 4-6 hours. It should no longer be liquid but should leave a slight impression when pressed gently.

Curing and Demolding

  1. Full cure: 24-72 hours for full cure at 75°F. Cooler temperatures extend cure time; warmer temperatures shorten it. Do not move or disturb the mold during this period.

  2. Check readiness: After 24 hours, flex the silicone mold. If the resin feels room-temperature and firm, it’s ready for demolding. If still slightly warm, allow more time.

  3. Demold carefully: Flex the silicone mold broadly to release the resin blank. If the soap dish sticks, place the mold in the freezer for 10-15 minutes—cold contracts the silicone and releases the piece. Do not pry with hard objects.

  4. Clean edges: If resin squeezed out during pouring, clean edges with a craft knife or sandpaper after demolding.

Sanding, Polishing, and Finishing

  1. Initial sanding: Start with 400-grit wet/dry sandpaper, sanding the entire surface wet. Progress to 600-grit, keeping the surface wet to avoid heat buildup. For a smoother finish, progress to 800-grit.

  2. Polishing: Apply a small amount of automotive polishing compound to a microfiber cloth. Buff in circular motions until the surface is smooth and glossy.

  3. Seal and protect: Apply a clear acrylic sealer or UV-resistant clear coat for pieces that will face windows or outdoor elements. For indoor use alone, the polished resin surface is sufficiently durable.

  4. Prepare for use: Ensure the drainage channel is clear of resin debris. The soap dish is now ready for bathroom use.

FAQ

Q: How long before I can use my resin soap dish? A: 72 hours for full cure at 75°F. For best results, wait 7 days before placing wet soap on the surface to ensure complete cure. If the dish has a drainage channel, wait full cure time before exposing to standing water.

Q: Can I use regular acrylic paint on my resin piece? A: No. Acrylics contain water and will cure improperly, causing cloudiness or separation. Use only pigment powders or liquid resin dyes formulated for epoxy.

Q: Will my resin soap dish yellow over time? A: Epoxy resin can yellow after 1-2 years in direct sunlight. UV-resistant pigments and annual clear-coat application slow yellowing. For best longevity, keep out of prolonged direct sun, especially on windowsills near light sources.

Q: Do I need a drainage hole in my resin soap dish? A: Yes, recommended. Drill a small channel after full cure and sand smooth to allow water to escape. Standing water will eventually cloud or degrade the resin interior. If no drainage channel, use the dish for decorative display only, not for wet soap.

Q: Can I make resin soap dishes without a silicone mold? A: Yes—use a plastic tray as a negative mold, carve foam, or 3D-print a form. Silicone molds give the best detail and easiest demolding, but alternatives work in a pinch.

Troubleshooting Facts Verified from Practice

  • A 1:1 weight ratio mix that cures tacky in 6-8 hours at 75°F will remain tacky 24+ hours if ratio is off by just 3%—always verify with a digital scale accurate to 0.1g.
  • Epoxy resin soap dish walls 1/4″ thick cure fully in 24-48 hours at 75°F; thinner walls cure faster, thicker walls may remain tacky or overheat.
  • Mica powders show minimal fading after 6 months in direct sunlight; certain organic pigment powders may fade noticeably and are best for pieces away from windows.
  • Silicone mold putty typically captures details at 1:1 ratio by weight; mixing inaccuracies show as streaks or tacky spots in the finished piece.
  • Pieces demolded from silicone molds after 24-hour cure release cleanly without force; premature demolding causes tearing or distortion.
  • A tacky surface after 72 hours at proper temperature almost always indicates an inaccurate mix ratio or insufficient mixing—the resin-rich or hardener-rich side of the mix ratio didn’t react fully.

Bottom Line

Resin soap dishes and bathroom accessories are practical DIY projects that combine function with personalization—your bathroom wears custom pieces that reflect your style. The key success factors are precise mix ratio by weight (always use a digital scale accurate to 0.1g), working at the resin’s tacky stage (4-6 hours at 75°F) for embedments and additional pours, and allowing full 72-hour cure before use. Start with a simple shape and one color before layering effects. Each piece takes 3 days from start to finish, but the result is a durable, attractive addition your bathroom will enjoy.
