Soft Spots Cured Resin Repair

How to identify, repair, and prevent soft spots in cured epoxy and polyurethane resin projects

Sep 27, 2026

Soft Spots Cured Resin Repair

When resin cures with soft spots, the problem is usually incomplete curing, incorrect mixing, or environmental contamination during the pour. This guide covers how to diagnose, repair, and prevent soft spots so your projects cure uniformly every time.

Why Soft Spots Occur

Inaccurate Mixing Ratio

Epoxy and polyurethane resins depend on precise weight ratios between base and hardener. Even small deviations throw off the chemical reaction, leaving uncured pockets that feel soft or tacky to the touch. Using volume measurements instead of weight is the most common cause.

Incomplete Mixing

Resin and hardener must be stirred thoroughly for the recommended time, scraping the sides and bottom of the mixing container. Uneven mixing leaves localized areas with too much base or too much hardener, both of which fail to cure fully.

Temperature Too Cold

Resin cure reactions slow dramatically below 70°F (21°C). At temperatures below 65°F (18°C), the cure may not complete within the expected timeframe, resulting in soft spots that never harden even after several days.

Contamination

Skin oils, dust, or solvent residue on the mold surface or in the mixing container can inhibit curing. Amine blush from polyurethane resins can also create a waxy layer that prevents complete cure in affected areas.

Diagnosing Soft Spots

Touch Test

After the recommended cure time (typically 24–72 hours depending on system), press firmly on the surface. Soft spots feel noticeably softer than the surrounding cured resin, often with a slight give or tackiness. Document the exact locations.

Scrape Test

Lightly scrape the surface with a putty knife or razor blade. Soft spots will show material coming away easily, while fully cured resin requires significant pressure to remove anything. Compare multiple areas.

Pin Prick Test

Prick the soft spot with a pin. If the material compresses or the pin penetrates without resistance, the spot is uncured or under-cured. Fully cured resin will not yield.

Repair Methods

Light Sanding and Top Coat

For minor soft spots (limited to the surface third of the cure depth):

  1. Sand the affected area with 80-grit sandpaper to remove the uncured material
  2. Clean thoroughly with 91% isopropyl alcohol
  3. Apply a new mixed coat of resin per the manufacturer’s instructions
  4. Allow full cure before handling

This method works when the soft spot is superficial and the underlying structure is sound.

Localized Resin Infusion

For soft spots deeper than the surface layer:

  1. Grind out the soft spot to create a V-shaped channel, removing all uncured material
  2. Clean the channel with alcohol and allow to dry completely
  3. Mix a small batch of fresh resin and hardener
  4. Infuse the channel using a syringe or small brush, overfilling slightly
  5. Cover and allow full cure (24+ hours) before sanding flush

Full Surface Re-coat

When soft spots are widespread:

  1. Grind the entire surface to remove all soft material
  2. Clean thoroughly with alcohol and allow to dry
  3. Apply a complete fresh coat of mixed resin
  4. Maintain optimal temperature (70°F–75°F) and humidity (below 65%)
  5. Allow full cure before use

Troubleshooting Specific Scenarios

Soft Spots on Edges

Edge soft spots often result from inadequate mixing at the container edges or temperature gradients during cure. Grind the edges, clean, and apply a new coat. For future projects, mix in a larger container and pour in thinner layers.

Soft Spots Under Decorative Elements

When soft spots appear under embeds (flowers, pigments, objects), the embed may have displaced resin or trapped air preventing cure. Remove the embed, grind the area, and re-infuse with fresh resin. Seal porous embeds before placement.

Recurring Soft Spots

If soft spots reappear after repair, the root cause (mixing technique, temperature, or contamination) remains unaddressed. Review each step: weigh base and hardener on a calibrated scale, mix for the full recommended time, and maintain shop temperature above 70°F.

Prevention Checklist

  • Weigh base and hardener on a calibrated digital scale (0.1g precision)
  • Mix for the full recommended time, scraping sides and bottom every 20 seconds
  • Maintain shop temperature between 70°F–75°F (21°C–24°C)
  • Keep humidity below 65% during pour and cure
  • Clean molds with 91% isopropyl alcohol before each use
  • Apply a compatible release agent before every pour
  • Mix in larger batches only if you can stir thoroughly for the full duration
  • Protect poured resin from dust and temperature drafts during the cure period

FAQ

Q: Can I fix soft spots without sanding the entire surface?
A: Yes — if the soft spots are limited to one or two small areas, you can grind out only those spots and apply a localized top coat. Match the mixed ratio and cure conditions exactly to the original pour.

Q: How long after the original pour can I repair soft spots?
A: If the resin is fully cured (7+ days), you can grind and re-coat at any time. If the resin is still within its initial cure window (24–72 hours), the new mix may chemically bond with the existing layer. For older pieces, mechanical removal and a fresh coat is required.

Q: Will a heat gun fix soft spots?
A: No — applying heat to under-cured resin will not complete the chemical reaction and may worsen the soft spot by creating additional heat buildup. Remove the uncured material mechanically and apply fresh resin.

Q: Do soft spots mean the resin is defective?
A: Not necessarily. Soft spots typically result from mixing errors, temperature issues, or contamination rather than a faulty product. Following the mixing and environmental guidelines in this guide prevents the vast majority of cases.

Q: Can I prevent soft spots by using a different hardener ratio for faster cure?
A: No — deviating from the manufacturer’s specified ratio will not speed up cure reliably and will almost certainly introduce soft spots or other cure failures. Always use the exact ratio specified for your resin system.

Bottom Line

Soft spots in cured resin stem from mixing inaccuracies, inadequate mixing, cold temperatures, or contamination. Surface-level soft spots can be repaired by grinding and applying a fresh top coat. Deeper spots require localized infusion or full surface re-coating. Prevention depends on precise weighing, thorough mixing, and maintaining optimal shop conditions (70°F–75°F, below 65% humidity). Always follow the manufacturer’s mixing ratios and times — deviating these is the fastest path to cure failures.
