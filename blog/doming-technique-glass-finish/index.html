Doming technique glass finish for resin art: step-by-step guide to achieving a flawless domed glass-like surface on canvas and wood panels

Sep 27, 2026

Doming Technique Glass Finish

The doming technique creates a raised, glass-like dome over your resin art, adding depth and a professional finish that catches light beautifully. This guide walks through every step to achieve a perfect domed surface on canvas or wood panels.

What Is the Doming Technique?

Doming involves pouring a specialized resin mixture that self-levels into a convex, bubble-free dome over your cured base layer. The result is a 3D glass effect that appears to float above the artwork surface. This technique is popular for creating focal points, simulating water droplets, or adding a final protective layer with optical clarity.

Materials and Ratios

Material Quantity Notes

Hardener 500ml 1:1 mixing ratio by volume
Mixing cups 100ml minimum Disposable silicone cups work best
Stir sticks 10 per batch Wooden or plastic, never metal
Heat gun or torch 1 For bubble removal
Dust cover 1 Keeps debris off wet resin
gloves box of 100 Nitrile, powder-free
Safety glasses 1 pair Required for eye protection

Critical ratio: 1:1 resin to hardener by volume. Deviating will cause incomplete cure, sticky surface, or excessive heat generation.

Step-by-Step Process

Step 1: Prepare the Base Layer

Your base resin piece must be fully cured before doming. Minimum cure time is 72 hours at 75°F (24°C). The surface should be lightly sanded with 400-grit sandpaper and wiped clean with isopropyl alcohol (91% or higher). Any dust, oil, or residue will prevent proper adhesion.

Step 2: Mix the Doming Resin

Measure equal parts resin and hardener by volume. For a standard doming pour, 500ml total (250ml resin + 250ml hardener) covers a 8×10 inch canvas with 1/8 inch thickness. Mix slowly for 3 minutes, scraping the sides and bottom of the cup. Avoid incorporating air — stir gently, not vigorously.

Step 3: The Pour

Pour the mixed resin slowly from the center outward, allowing it to self-level. For a dome shape, pour slightly less than the full coverage amount — you can always add another thin layer. The resin will begin to dome naturally within 2-3 minutes as it gels.

Step 4: Bubble Removal

Within the first 5 minutes, use a heat gun on low setting or a butane torch to pass quickly across the surface. Do not hold the flame in one spot — keep moving to avoid local overheating. You should see bubbles pop within 10-15 seconds of heat application.

Step 5: The Cure

Cover the piece with a dust cover and leave undisturbed at 75°F (24°C) for 24 hours. Full cure reaches hardness at 7 days. Do not move or lean the piece against anything for the first 12 hours, as the soft dome can be deformed.

Troubleshooting

Problem Cause Fix
Fish eyes (craters) Surface contamination (oil, silicone) Clean with alcohol; avoid touching surface after prep
Bubbles returning after heat Over-mixing introduced air Mix more gently next time; use heat gun within 5-minute window
Soft or tacky surface Under-mixed ratio or cold temperature Maintain 75°F minimum; mix full 3 minutes scraping sides
Yellowing over time UV exposure without protection Use UV-resistant resin formula; display away from direct sunlight
Dome not forming Too much resin volume Reduce pour amount; build up with thin successive layers
Dust embedded Debris landing during cure Use dust cover from step 1; work in filtered environment

FAQ

Q: Can I dome over an already-finished piece with texture? A: Yes, but the texture will show through the clear dome. For best results, sand the surface smooth before the doming pour.

Q: How thick can a single doming pour be? A: Maximum 1/4 inch (3mm) per pour. Thicker pours generate excessive heat and may crack. Build desired height in 2-3 thin layers, allowing each to gel before the next.

Q: Do I need a special resin for doming? A: Low-viscosity, self-leveling epoxy formulated for doming or coating yields the best results. Standard casting resin can work but may require multiple thin passes.

Q: How long before I can hang or display the piece? A: Wait at least 24 hours before light handling, 72 hours before hanging. Full hardness at 7 days.

Q: Can I add pigment to the doming layer? A: Yes, but use resin-compatible pigments no more than 3% by volume. Excess pigment disrupts the self-leveling action and may prevent proper dome formation.

Q: What if my dome develops a slight curve instead of a perfect dome? A: This typically results from uneven base surface or inconsistent pour thickness. Ensure your base is level and pour from the center using a steady stream.

Bottom Line

The doming technique delivers a glass-like dome that elevates resin art from flat to dimensional. Success hinges on precise 1:1 mixing, clean surface prep, and patience — let each layer gel before assessing whether additional pours are needed. The key variables you can control are mix time, temperature (keep 75°F/24°C minimum), and dust exclusion. Everything else — bubble pattern, clarity, dome height — follows from those foundations.
