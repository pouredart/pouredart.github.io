Resin Art Business Basics: Etsy vs Local Sales

You can sell resin art online through a marketplace like Etsy or locally through craft fairs, consignment shops, and direct commissions. The channels differ in fees, effort, margins, and how fast you get paid. This guide compares them with real numbers so you can pick a starting channel instead of guessing.

The Fee Math, Side by Side

Etsy’s published fee structure (verify current rates before listing — they change):

  • Listing fee: $0.20 per listing, charged whether or not it sells; listings last 4 months or until sold.
  • Transaction fee: 6.5% of the item price plus shipping.
  • Payment processing: 3% + $0.25 per transaction (US Etsy Payments).
  • Offsite ads fee: if a buyer finds you through Etsy’s offsite advertising, an additional 12–15% on that sale (15% until you hit $10,000 in trailing-12-month sales, then 12%; above $10k it becomes mandatory).

Worked example: a $45 set of resin coasters with $8 shipping sold via Etsy with offsite ads at 15%:

  • Listing: $0.20
  • Transaction (6.5% of $53): $3.45
  • Processing (3% + $0.25): $1.84
  • Offsite ads (15% of $53): $7.95
  • Total fees: $13.44 — roughly 25% of the $53 collected.

Without the offsite-ads hit, fees drop to about $5.49, or ~10%.

Local channels have different costs:

  • Craft fair booth: typically $40–$150 per day for a community fair; $150–$400+ for established juried shows. Add a tent, table, and display racks one time (~$150–300 used).
  • Consignment shop: the shop takes 30–50% of the sale price; you keep the rest, and they handle the transaction. Payout is usually monthly.
  • Direct commissions (Instagram, Facebook, word of mouth): zero platform fees. You may pay ~2.9% + $0.30 if you take card payment through a portable reader or invoicing app; cash and bank transfer are free.

Same $45 coaster set at a $75/day craft fair: if you sell 6 sets that day ($270), booth cost is 28% of revenue — but if you sell 15 sets ($675), it drops to 11%. Local margins scale with foot traffic conversion; online margins are fixed per sale but you never pay for “air” on a slow day.

Startup Costs: What You Actually Need First

Before either channel, the product costs apply. A realistic first inventory run:

  • Resin and hardener for ~30 coaster sets or ~100 small jewelry pieces: $60–120 depending on brand and volume
  • Molds: $30–80 for a starter spread
  • Pigments, inks, glitter: $30–60
  • Safety gear (respirator, nitrile gloves, ventilation): $50–90 — non-negotiable, see Resin Art Safety Guide
  • Packaging: boxes, tissue, cards, $2–4 per coaster set, $0.50–1.50 per jewelry piece

That is roughly $200–450 to hold sellable inventory. Channel costs stack on top: Etsy needs maybe $20–40 in listing fees to open 100–200 listings plus photography time; a first craft fair needs the $75–150 booth fee plus display gear. Local sales can start cheaper if you begin with commissions — zero inventory risk, deposit in hand before you pour. Full cost accounting is in Resin Art Cost Breakdown: First Year.

Time Cost: The Hidden Fee

Etsy is front-loaded: photography, listing copy, tags, and SEO take 1–2 hours per product line, then the shop runs mostly passively — but you answer messages, renew listings, and pack orders on an unpredictable schedule. Early shops often see their first organic sale 4–12 weeks after listing; treat anything faster as luck, not a plan.

Local is event-loaded: a craft fair is 8–10 hours on-site plus setup/teardown, and you earn only while attending. Consignment is the lowest-effort local channel — drop off stock, collect monthly — but you wait 30+ days for cash and unsold pieces sit dead for months.

Commissions flip the model: 1–3 hours of customer conversation, a 50% deposit up front, then a scheduled pour. No inventory risk, highest effective hourly rate, and each delivered piece markets you to that customer’s circle.

Pricing: Don’t Guess

Whichever channel, price from cost, not from what you hope someone pays. The floor formula that keeps hobby businesses alive:

Price = (materials + packaging) × 2.5–3 + (hours × your hourly target) + channel fee buffer

The channel buffer matters: a piece priced at $40 “flat” nets you ~$36 on Etsy without offsite ads, ~$30 with them, ~$20–28 at consignment, and the full $40 direct. If you price once and sell everywhere, you are subsidizing the marketplace channels with your direct sales. Either price per channel or add the buffer into one global price. Step-by-step math with worked examples is in How To Price Resin Art To Sell.

Product-Channel Fit

Not every resin product suits every channel:

  • Coasters, trays, serving boards: strong at craft fairs (buyers see gloss and weight in person) and strong on Etsy (shippable flat, low breakage with $2–3 of packaging). The workhorse of both channels.
  • Jewelry (earrings, pendants): best on Etsy — tiny shipping cost, broad search demand, impulse price point ($15–45). Locally, jewelry sells slowly unless you have a display that stops people.
  • Custom/commission pours (pet memorials, wedding favors, logo pieces): direct and local only. Etsy’s structure punishes one-off custom work with its fees and review velocity expectations.
  • Large or heavy pieces (wall art, big trays): local and direct; shipping a 16” resin tray costs $20–40 insured and eats the margin unless priced in.
  • Seasonal items (ornaments, Halloween pieces): craft fairs in October–December convert exceptionally well; Etsy Q4 search volume also peaks, but competition from established shops is brutal for new sellers.

Reviews, Proof, and the Cold-Start Problem

Etsy buyers trust listings with 25+ reviews; getting the first 10 sales is the hardest phase of the whole business. Tactics that work: price early listings at a modest introductory margin, deliver fast (ship within 1–2 business days), and include a printed thank-you card asking for a review. Do not buy reviews or trade reviews — Etsy suspends shops for it.

Local selling has the reverse problem: zero cold start (walk-up buyers don’t check review counts) but zero compounding (each fair starts from scratch). Consignment and commissions build a local reputation slowly; an Etsy review count compounds forever. The two channels solve each other’s weaknesses, which is why most sustainable resin businesses run both — Etsy for reach and proof, local for margin and cash flow.

When To Choose Which

Start local/direct if: you have any access to fairs or a social circle that buys gifts; you need cash within 30 days; your pieces are large, heavy, or custom. Take deposits on commissions (50% up front is standard) and you eliminate inventory risk entirely.

Start Etsy if: your product is small and shippable (jewelry, coasters); you can tolerate 1–3 months before meaningful sales; you want a store that earns while you sleep once reviews accumulate.

Do both once one works: list your best local sellers on Etsy with photos taken at the fair, and bring Etsy screenshots of reviews to the next fair’s jury application. Each channel’s proof feeds the other.

Troubleshooting

  • Listings get views but no sales on Etsy. Price or photography problem, in that order. Compare your listing photo against page-one competitors for the same search term; if theirs is brighter and closer-cropped, redo yours. If photos match, you are priced above the trust threshold for a zero-review shop.
  • Craft fair: lots of compliments, few sales. Compliment traffic is real but converts at maybe 2–5%. Check three things: visible prices on everything (unpriced items suppress sales dramatically), a vertical display (flat tables halve attention), and at least one under-$20 item as an entry purchase.
  • Consignment shop hasn’t paid in 60 days. Get the agreement in writing before dropping stock: payout date, unsold-return terms, who insures breakage. If a shop is late twice, pull your inventory — consignment stock is an unsecured loan to the shop.
  • Custom client keeps changing the design after the deposit. Require a written spec and a single free revision in your deposit terms. Resin pours cannot be undone; scope changes after pour date get a new quote.
  • You are “busy” but not profitable. Track hours honestly including photos, listings, fair time, and cleaning. If your effective hourly is under ~$10, raise prices or cut the lowest-margin product — see the pricing floor formula above.

FAQ

Q: Is it legal to sell resin art from home? A: Generally yes as a sole proprietor, but rules vary by country and municipality. You typically need to register for self-employment/taxes, and some locales require a home-business permit. Resin items that contact food (cutting boards, serving trays) face additional food-safety rules — most makers label them decorative or serve-on-tray only.

Q: What sells best for a new resin business? A: Coasters and small jewelry are the standard starters: low material cost ($3–8 per set/pair), fast production, and demand on both channels. Custom and memorial pieces earn the most per hour but need an existing reputation. See First 5 Resin Projects Ranked By Difficulty for skill progression before you commit inventory money.

Q: How much should I charge for a set of 4 resin coasters? A: Common market range is $30–60 depending on complexity and region. Compute your floor with the formula above; if materials plus packaging are $10 and a set takes 45 minutes active time, $40–45 is a sane starting price that survives Etsy fees.

Q: Do I need a separate business bank account? A: Yes, the day you take your first sale. Commingled accounts make tax time miserable and pierce the clean record you need if a marketplace or payment processor asks for verification. A free personal-bank business sub-account is enough at first.

Q: Which channel gives faster cash? A: Local, decisively. A craft fair pays the same day; a commission deposit pays before you make the piece. Etsy pays on its deposit schedule (typically 1–5 days after sale) but only after the cold-start period produces sales at all.

Bottom Line

Etsy charges roughly 10–25% per sale depending on offsite ads, gives you compounding reviews and passive reach, but has a 1–3 month cold start; local selling has booth fees instead of commissions, pays same-day, and converts big and custom pieces that ship badly. Most resin businesses need both, but start where your cash-clock and product type point: local/commissions for large pieces and fast cash, Etsy for jewelry and coasters. Price once with the channel fee baked in, track real hours, and let each channel’s proof feed the other.
